## 6. Likely questions (answers based only on the repo)

**1. Why does Uzbek need its own inventory instead of translating TurBLiMP?**
About half of TurBLiMP's 16 phenomena don't carry over as they are. Turkish aorist allomorphy has no Uzbek counterpart, the honorific plural makes the Turkish subject-agreement design unreliable, and for impersonal passives my judgment is the opposite of Turkish (the version without an agent is the bad one). Uzbek also has its own clean contrasts, like no plural after numerals and possessive agreement, so a translation would produce "bad" sentences that are acceptable and miss the Uzbek ones. *(notes/phenomena.md, rows 7, 11, 15, 17, 22)*

**2. How will you validate the pairs?**
Every pair gets a native-speaker check; I've already gone through 20 of the 22 phenomena and corrected several. TurBLiMP showed its 30 raters only about 6 pairs per phenomenon, so checking all ~1,000 of ours by hand is something we can do better. On top of that, I'd like Uzbek linguists to review the phenomenon guide and, if possible, run a small Likert judgment study with informed consent like TurBLiMP's. *(notes/turblimp.md, notes/phenomena.md)*

**3. How does the alphabet comparison separate tokenization effects from grammar?**
Latin vs Cyrillic uses the two Goldfish models trained on each script: neither produces unknown tokens on its own script, and both get 13/13 in the pilot. For reformed Latin I'd only use models whose tokenizer encodes ö, ğ, ş, ç without unknown tokens, report token counts next to accuracy, and report separately the pairs where the tested letter itself changes. That doesn't remove tokenization effects completely, but it makes them visible instead of hiding them inside accuracy. *(notes/pilot_3alphabets.md)*

**4. How many pairs are enough?**
With 50 pairs, Qwen's 82% has a 95% range of roughly ±11 points (71–93%), so small differences mean nothing. At about 100 pairs per phenomenon it's roughly ±8 points, which is why I'm aiming for 50–100 pairs each across 10–15 phenomena and will report bootstrap confidence intervals. *(log.md, notes/turblimp.md)*

**5. What happens if a phenomenon has too few corpus sentences?**
The corpus is for finding natural candidates; it isn't required, because TurBLiMP also wrote its seed pairs by hand and then varied the words. The three phenomena counted so far have plenty (58,768 sentences for *-dan keyin*, 31,152 for *hech* with negation), and one ~20-minute run would count the others. The corpus terms aren't clear yet either, so I'd use corpus sentences as models rather than release them verbatim. *(results/corpus_stats.json, notes/corpus.md)*
